# Report Structure

The report is written **for a fellow technical interaction designer**. It is not a scientific report — no citations, no theory. Just your solutions, and why they are the ones you chose.

The body carries **decisions and their consequences**. Artefacts — wireframes, raw session notes, anything you produced along the way — go in the appendix. An examiner reading this on a plane wants to know what you learned and what you changed because of it; they will look at the screens if they want to check you.

Which means it has to stand on its own. Link to your Figma if you have one, but a link is a supplement, never the only copy: if it is needed to follow the argument, it is in here.

## Sections

- **Title page** — project name, team members, names and emails.

- **Project description** (one page) — what you set out to achieve, for whom, and the one twist that makes it yours rather than a generic to-do.

- **The user study** — your population, how many people you spoke to, the themes that came out of it, and the prioritised tasks you derived. The deliverable you handed in, as it ended up rather than as it started.

- **What the testing changed** (one or two pages) — you ran think-alouds on the **wireframes**, before any of it was built. So: which tasks you asked people to do, what went wrong, and what you redrew because of it. Findings and decisions here; the screens themselves and your session notes go in the appendix.

  A finding nobody acted on is worth reporting too, as long as you say why not.

- **The data model** (one page) — the ER diagram, and a paragraph on the parts of it that were not obvious: what you made a pointer and what you made a relation, where sharing lives, anything you modelled one way and then changed.

- **Visual design and accessibility** (one or two pages) — the design language you chose and why, plus the accessibility audit and what it made you fix.

- **Implementation**
  - Link to the GitHub repository
  - Link to your Back4App database — invite the examiner accounts listed on learnIT
  - How the examiners run your code. Normally your repo's `README.md` already explains this. But if anything secret is needed — the contents of an `.env` file, app keys, configuration that is deliberately not committed — put it in the report.
  - If you have created accounts for the examiners to log in with, say so and give the credentials. (This makes sense for some applications and not others.)

- **Reflection** (two pages max) — looking back at the project:
  - What worked well?
  - What did using your own app teach you that the testing did not?
  - What could have been done better — by the team, and by the TID staff?

- **Appendix** — the wireframes, the think-aloud notes, and anything else you want on record. No page limit, and nobody expects it to be polished.
